\section*{1. General}
\label{sec:general}

\subsection*{Sample coverage and composition}

Our main dataset contains \GenTotalContracts\ PDFs, corresponding to \GenUniqueCAOs\ distinct CAOs. The cross-sectional scope is mostly sectoral: about \GenShareSectoral\ of CAOs are sectoral agreements.

Figure~\ref{fig:date_counts} shows the number of contracts per start year based on two alternative dates: the web-scraped start date (\texttt{ingangsdatum}) and the start date read from the PDF (\texttt{general\_start\_date}).

Coverage increases steeply from 2008 to the early 2010s and then stabilises between \GenStableLow\ and \GenStableHigh\ new contract episodes per year (\GenStableStartYear--\GenStableEndYear, PDF date). We observe a peak of \GenPeakCount\ contracts in \GenPeakYear. The sharp fall in 2025--2026 is a pure right-censoring effect. The two series lie almost on top of each other, so the assignment of contracts to start-year cohorts is essentially unaffected by whether we use website or PDF dates. Based on \texttt{ingangsdatum}, roughly \GenShareAvv\ of all contracts are AVV-covered (universally binding for an entire sector).

\subsection*{Date quality and discrepancies}

Table~\ref{tab:general_summary} reports missingness and discrepancies for the two start-date variables. All \GenTotalContracts\ contracts have \texttt{ingangsdatum}, and \GenBothDatesCount\ contracts have both website and PDF dates observed. Exactly \GenExactDateMatch\ of contracts show an exact match between the website and PDF start dates, with only \GenDateDiffGtThirty\ exhibiting a date discrepancy greater than 30 days.

\subsection*{Document type and truncation}

After excluding non--full CAO files, \GenFullCAOCount\ documents are used for core analysis. The excluded files consist of partial amendments, other supplements, annexes, and procedure documents. Across the sample, \GenTruncatedFiles\ files hit the extraction token limit and were truncated, spanning \GenTruncatedCaos\ unique CAO numbers. Truncation is concentrated in a limited number of agreements rather than being evenly distributed across the sample (Table~\ref{tab:truncation_concentration}).

\subsection*{General institutional provisions}

Figure~\ref{fig:provisions_latest} summarises key institutional features of the CAOs: AVV coverage, retroactive application, explicit retroactive backpay, interim amendment clauses (TTW), and company-level deviation clauses in the latest-CAO cross-section. Figure~\ref{fig:provisions_panel} shows the same measures across the contract-episode panel.

Company-level deviation clauses are common but not universal: across \GenStableStartYear--\GenStableEndYear, the yearly share of CAOs allowing some form of firm-level deviation from the sectoral standard ranges from \GenDevShareMin\ to \GenDevShareMax. Retroactive clauses are less common: pooling all years, approximately \GenShareRetro\ of contracts have a retroactive start date. Among those, \GenShareBackpayCond\ explicitly require retroactive backpay (with a median retroactivity period of \GenMedianRetroDays\ days), while interest or surcharges on backpay remain extremely rare (\GenShareSurcharge). Interim amendment clauses (TTW) vary considerably by cohort, reaching up to \GenTTWShareMax\ of contracts in a given start year.
