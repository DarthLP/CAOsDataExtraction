The employer and employee can agree on a higher number of function steps or periodics. Upon employment, the employee receives at least the wage corresponding to the first function step. For Industrial bakeries: The fully qualified employee is placed in the first step upon employment. An employee is fully qualified if they meet all job requirements at the required pace, know what is expected, and can perform tasks independently. This is assessed via a performance review. An employee is fully qualified at the latest after accumulating 1,976 hours of experience in the same function. If not yet fully qualified, the employee remains at step 0. Progression within scales: The employee's function-hourly wage is increased annually by 1 periodic or function step on January 1, until the maximum of the scale is reached. If the employee starts employment, turns 21, or is promoted on or after October 1, they skip the first periodic increase on January 1. Performance-based step changes: The shaded periodics at the end of the wage scale may depend on a performance review. The review takes place before January 1. The employer can grant or withhold these periodics based on the review. If no review takes place, the employer grants the periodic. Personal allowance: The employer can provide the employee with a supplement to the function wage and allowances in the form of a personal allowance. This personal allowance is not increased by the collective wage increase, unless the employer and employee agree otherwise. The personal allowance resulting from Article 2.35.i or Article 4.25.i of the CAO Bakkerbedrijf 2012-2013 (ORBA-pt) is increased by the collective wage increase. Function change to lower scale: If placed in a function in a lower wage scale, the employer assigns a wage that is as close as possible to the employee's original function-hourly wage and allowances. If granting periodics is not sufficient, the difference is converted into a personal allowance. Older employee protection: An employee aged 55 until AOW-age, who moves to a lower-classified function at the employer's request, retains their original function-hourly wage. An employee aged 60 or older can request to be placed on a waiting list for day shift functions, provided they have worked regularly in shift work in the sector for the past 5 years. If placed in a day shift function, the employee retains their original wage. Function change due to reorganization: An employee who moves to a lower-classified function due to reorganization or merger retains their original function-hourly wage and employment conditions. Wage payment: Function wage and fixed allowances are paid by the end of the wage payment period. Variable allowances are paid by the end of the next wage payment period. A wage payment period is a maximum of one month. The employee receives a payslip with each wage payment, which can be digital with employee consent. Collective wage increases (Artisanal bakeries): 6% per December 1, 2023; 5% per June 1, 2024; 2.75% per January 1, 2025. Increases paid by individual employers before December 1, 2023, are not offset. The agreed 6% is paid per December 1, 2023. Deviating agreements made by unions with employers, or by employees with their employer, remain applicable if they are more favorable. Youth wage scales are increased by the same percentages and on the same dates. BHV allowance, company clothing allowance, cool and freezer cell allowance, and ORBA allowance are also increased by these percentages. Collective wage increases (Industrial bakeries): 7% per July 1, 2023; 4.5% per January 1, 2024; 4.5% per July 1, 2024. BHV allowance, company clothing allowance, cool and freezer cell allowance, and ORBA allowance are also increased by these percentages. Jubilee gratuity: The employee receives a gratuity upon a jubilee: 25 years of service: 100% of monthly wage. 40 years of service: 100% of monthly wage. Additionally, the employee is entitled to short-term absence leave as regulated in Article 31. The employer pays the gross monthly wage for 25 and 40 years of service net, as long as and to the extent that fiscal regulations allow. For a wage payment period of one week or four weeks, the gross wage is converted to a gross (monthly) wage. Study allowance for BBL apprentices: An employee younger than 21 years participating in vocational training (Wet Educatie en Beroepsonderwijs - WEB) receives a study allowance of 12.5% over the earned function wage in the weeks they attended lessons or were absent without fault. The study allowance is paid simultaneously with the first wage payment after the end of a study year, or immediately if the employee stops vocational training, turns 21, or the employment contract ends. Wage upon change of working hours pattern: If the employer changes the roster after it has been announced, the employee receives at least the wage they would have received based on the previously announced roster. If the employer places the employee in a different roster due to a reorganization, the employee receives an allowance if they worked at least 3 years in the old roster and lose the right to an allowance for working at specific hours. The allowance is 50% of the loss and is paid for 6 months. Company clothing allowance (Artisanal bakeries): If the employer does not provide company clothing to logistics, production, and technical service personnel, the employee receives a net allowance. The allowance is dependent on the number of hours worked: <10 hours no allowance, 10 to 20 hours 50% of the allowance, >=20 hours 100% of the allowance. Company clothing allowance (Industrial bakeries): If the employer does not provide company clothing to logistics, production, and technical service personnel, the employee receives a net allowance. The allowance is dependent on the number of hours worked: <10 hours no allowance, 10 to 20 hours 50% of the allowance, >=20 hours 100% of the allowance. Delivery driver allowance: The employee with the function 'Delivery driver' receives an allowance of 15% of their function-hourly wage for hours worked on Saturday. BHV allowance (Artisanal bakeries): The employee appointed as an emergency response team member (BHV-er) by the employer receives a BHV allowance. BHV allowance (Industrial bakeries): The employee appointed as an emergency response team member (BHV-er) by the employer receives a BHV allowance. Vitality wage: The wage consists of function wage and gross allowances. The vitality wage is obtained by multiplying the adjusted wage (due to the scheme) by a factor of 1.125. Vitality wage = adjusted function wage/gross allowances x 1.125. The following gross allowances count towards determining the vitality wage: irregular hours allowance, overtime allowance, cool/freezer cell allowance, BHV allowance, personal allowance, holiday allowance. The ORBA allowance or an allowance from an individual agreement between employer and employee is generally adjusted to 80% unless employer and employee have agreed that the personal allowance is received regardless of the extent of working hours.
