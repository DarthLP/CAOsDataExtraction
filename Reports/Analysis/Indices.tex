\section*{4. Generosity Indices}
\label{sec:indices}

\subsection*{Index construction methodology and standardization}

We now construct standardized generosity indices across 13 core domains (12 non-salary amenities plus wages), following the empirical framework in \texttt{indices/METHODOLOGY.md}. To ensure comparability across diverse contract durations and calendar years, the pipeline applies \textbf{pooled-z} standardization:
\begin{equation}
    z_{it} = \frac{x_{it} - \mu_{\text{yardstick}}}{\sigma_{\text{yardstick}}}
\end{equation}

To prevent frequently renegotiated or voluminous agreements from distorting the baseline, yardstick parameters $(\mu, \sigma)$ are estimated on a de-duplicated subsample containing exactly one representative contract per term-group (\texttt{cao\_number} + \texttt{ingangsdatum}, selecting the latest publication). Raw numeric provisions are winsorized to the 1st and 99th percentiles ($[P_1, P_{99}]$) of the yardstick distribution to insulate against optical scan errors, and standardized scores are subsequently hard-clipped to $[-3.0, +3.0]$ to cap extreme tail leverage during composite aggregation.

For each topic, two distinct operational tracks are scored:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Magnitude track}: standardized intensity of provisions where present (e.g., wage levels, vacation days, overtime surcharge percentages, sick leave wage continuation rates).
    \item \textbf{Coverage track}: the breadth of codified worker protections (e.g., the proportion of potential fringe benefits, psychosocial health protocols, or flexible contract protections provided).
\end{enumerate}
The topic-level combined score represents the unweighted mean of its standardized magnitude and coverage tracks. The overall agreement-level generosity index (\texttt{overall\_z}) is computed as an equal-weighted composite across all eligible domains. 

In parallel, an institutional statutory benchmark (``pseudo-CAO'') is continuously tracked across time, scoring mandatory legal statutory baselines under Dutch labor law (including WML, statutory vacation entitlements under Art.~7:634 BW, WAZO parental leaves, and statutory transition severance under Art.~7:673 BW).

\subsection*{Sample filtering and cross-chapter reconciliation}

Sample counts across the report vary systematically according to the underlying unit of observation and analytical filter:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{2,739 Raw Records vs.\ 2,698 Full-CAO Documents}: The core generosity pipeline filters strictly on complete collective agreements (\texttt{full\_cao\_original}, $N = 2,079$, and \texttt{full\_cao\_update}, $N = 619$), yielding \IndCompositeTotalDocs\ documents. It deliberately excludes 41 partial amendments, annexes, and procedural protocols. In collective bargaining law, an interim amendment only recites modified terms; treating unmentioned clauses in an amendment as absent would artificially collapse that document's generosity score.
    \item \textbf{2,444 Wage-Matched Documents}: Exactly 2,444 full-CAO documents match to an observable wage scale in \texttt{mw\_indices.csv} (Table~\ref{tab:indices_pay_correlations}); the remaining 254 documents lack parsed salary tables.
\end{itemize}
